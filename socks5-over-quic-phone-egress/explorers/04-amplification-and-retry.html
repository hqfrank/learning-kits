The 3× limit caps what a spoofed Initial can reflect; a Retry token cuts it below 1× and keeps the handshake slots for real phones

The cloud's QUIC port accepts UDP from anywhere. An attacker can send Initial packets with a victim's address as the source. Until a client proves it receives packets at its address, a server may send at most × the bytes it received from it (RFC 9000 §8.1). The design goes further: it answers every new Initial with a small stateless Retry that carries a token, and starts the TLS handshake only when the token comes back from the claimed address (RFC 9000 §8.1.2). Move the sizes and the flood rate (notes C1, B3, B2).

Protocol limits are public (RFC 9000, RFC 9001). The flight sizes, token length, flood rate and the time an abandoned handshake holds a slot are page choices, illustrative; the handshake cap is an illustrative design parameter. The Retry size is computed from its fields with worst-case 20-byte connection IDs. Nothing here is a measurement.
