01|A home robot is not one program. It is a crowd of services on one small computer, all at once. A camera makes depth at thirty hertz. People perception runs near ten. Mapping, voice, and a control loop at fifty hertz on isolated cores. They share one C P U and one G P U.
02|The benchmark answers two questions for this stack on this computer. Does it all fit at once? And when something gives, what runs out first — the C P U, the G P U, or memory? A single average utilisation number cannot tell you whether the robot still walks while it thinks.
03|Predict before you measure. Write a budget ledger: time equals arithmetic over the compute rate, plus bytes over the bandwidth, plus C P U overhead. A prediction made before the data cannot be tuned to fit it. And it warns you: a small batch-one model does little math per launch. Like a van stuck at every traffic light, it spends its time between kernels, not computing. It is launch-bound, far slower than its roofline.
04|Measure each service alone first, at three levels. L one is the bare engine. L two is the service as deployed, with its real glue. L three adds the real upstream feeding it. The gaps are the finding: L two minus L one is the service's own C P U glue, and that glue usually costs more than the network. And an event-driven service with no input shows an idle floor, near zero, not its working cost. You must drive it.
05|Now the bench. Everything that stands in for the world — fake motor boards, a fake microphone, cloud stubs, synthetic people — runs on a second machine. If the harness ran on the robot, it would steal the very cycles you are trying to measure. A dedicated link carries the control bus; a separate link carries the bulk feeds.
06|The bench can lie in confident ways. A shared link lets bulk traffic slow the control bus below its rate. Device interrupts all land on core zero and disturb the loop. Turning on tracing can add milliseconds to the very latency it measures. And a benchmark fed all-zero data runs faster than real data, because the datapath never toggles. A clean number can still be wrong.
07|To stop yourself tuning a result, freeze the rules first. Warm up, then measure a fixed window. Before its numbers count, gates check the window: services alive, rates as expected, no crash, no stray C P U, clocks locked at both ends. Conditions run interleaved, so slow drift cancels. Hypotheses and thresholds are pre-registered, so a result cannot be tuned into passing.
08|Finally, run the whole stack together. The combined cost beats the sum of the solos, because the services fight over C P U, G P U, and memory. That ratio is the interference factor. Each timing promise is judged pass, unresolved, or fail, and a pre-set rule names the first resource to give. On a small robot computer, it is often the C P U quota that fills before the G P U.
