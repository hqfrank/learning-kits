Wi-Fi CSI sensing · story 1

Wi-Fi feels a moving person the way a spider feels a fly. One spider knows something moved; it usually takes several to say where.A moving person makes the CSI fluctuate. One link can sense that motion happened; it usually takes several devices to say where.

The radio waves in a home bounce along many paths at once — think of them as one big web. The router is the hub spider; each sensing device is a listener spider. A person who moves is a fly: the threads near it tremble. Flip to Engineer view at any time for the real names.

3But every web hums differentlyFeatures must not depend on the room

Where the web breaks

  • Radio waves need no threads. They fill the whole room and pass through walls; the "web" is only the set of paths.
  • A fly touches the web. A person touches no path: they reflect, block and diffract the waves near them.
  • A real spider feels a still fly's weight. CSI barely sees a still person: only breathing and absorption remain (C5).
  • The "notes" are not sound. Subcarriers are radio tones {{ofdm_subcarrier_spacing_11a_n_ac}} kHz apart, all sent at once in every frame.
  • Spiders "comparing" threads is computation. Device-to-device links cost power and must follow the AP when it changes channel (B4).
