A longer window and a stricter rule mean fewer false alarms and later decisions, and the ACF keeps its threshold when the room changes but variance does not

A synthetic single-link CSI stream follows a scripted day: a walk near the device, small motion, a 4 s walk, someone walking in the next room, and optionally a fan in the empty home. A sliding window computes one feature, the lag-1 autocorrelation (ACF) or the variance, averaged over subcarriers (B2). A threshold and an "N windows in a row" rule turn it into decisions (B3, C1). The page scores each scenario: detected or missed, time to detect, time to clear, and false alarms. It then converts the false-alarm count to alarms per month (C1).

Synthetic stream, not a measurement. The scenario amplitudes, the slow shadowing term, the fan and the noise floor are page assumptions assumption. Report rates and windows are illustrative values from constants.json illustrative. A 4-minute stream cannot show that a budget of a few false alarms per month is met (C2). It can only show the mechanism.

Sign convention. This page uses the mean-removed ACF, as in the motion statistic of Zhang et al. 2019 (WiDetect, cited in B2). A still room then gives a lag-1 ACF near 0, and motion raises it. Note B2 describes the opposite direction ("a still channel … close to 1"). That holds for the un-centred correlation of successive CSI reports. The two forms carry the same information with opposite signs. This difference is reported back for the note.
