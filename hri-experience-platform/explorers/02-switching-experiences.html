Whether an experience can run here is decided at start-up from the catalogs, and switching to another is a full restart

An experience does not list its capabilities. They are derived from the primitives and inputs it declares, through the primitive catalog and the input catalog, and every catalog entry names its targets (simulation, physical), each with its own adapter or producer contract (B5). Readiness fails unless every required input has a healthy producer and every primitive a healthy adapter on this target; a missing optional input may only select a declared degraded branch. Switch producers off and change the target to see which of three experiences is eligible. Then plan a switch: it is stop, clean, restart, never a hot swap during a visit (B5).

An illustrative model, not a measurement. The three experiences (a greeting, a chat, a wave) are the first-registry experiences of note C2; which inputs and primitives each declares, and the wave's optional speech input, are page choices that follow notes B3 and B5. The timings are illustrative design parameters from constants.json or page choices. The notes give no restart time for the physical robot, so the switch timeline uses the simulation host lifecycle (C1).
