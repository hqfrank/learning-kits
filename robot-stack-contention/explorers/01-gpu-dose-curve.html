The object detector's added GPU wait follows the other processes' share of GPU time, and each point costs more than the last

Without MPS the GPU runs one CUDA context at a time (time-slicing), so the object detector waits while the other processes hold their turns (contexts and time-slicing: nvidia-gpu-anatomy, note 07). The x-axis is the dose O: the other processes' share of GPU-active time, in percentage points, with the detector's own time removed. The y-axis is the detector's added blocked time per frame over the no-load window. Move O, then cut the VLA's share by a few points to see how much time that buys back (note B1).

Caveat first. All points are illustrative (class illustrative): round teaching numbers that keep the shape of the finding, not measurements. The curve comes from a reduced row (the depth camera service and the object detector only, idle CPU pool) with a compute co-tenant, one window per arm. In the full stack the mechanism is expected, not tested. The line between the arms is a linear interpolation, not a fitted law.
