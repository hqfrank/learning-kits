# ASD-STE100 at 80% — the writing rules learn-notes follows

ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English) is a controlled language for aerospace maintenance documentation. Applied fully it is too stringent for study notes; applied at roughly 80% it produces prose that is unusually easy to read. These are the rules that carry over.

## Keep (the 80%)

1. **One topic per paragraph, one instruction or fact per sentence.** Sentences ≤ 20 words in procedural text, ≤ 25 in descriptive text.
2. **Active voice.** "The copy engine shares the controller queue" not "the controller queue is shared by the copy engine".
3. **One word, one meaning; one meaning, one word.** Pick a term for each concept and never vary it. If the source uses two names, state the equivalence once in the glossary and use one name thereafter.
4. **Define before use.** A term appears in the glossary or is defined in the sentence where it first appears.
5. **Present tense for facts, simple past for what was measured.** "L2 latency is about 150 ns on both boards. The campaign measured 146 ns on Orin and 156 ns on Thor."
6. **Articles and determiners present.** No telegraphic style.
7. **No synonyms for variety, no idioms, no metaphors in the body text.** One analogy is allowed in a clearly marked "Analogy:" line per mechanism, then dropped.
8. **Numbers with units, every time.** "259 GB/s", never "high bandwidth".
9. **Lists for sequences and enumerations; prose for reasoning.** Do not turn an argument into bullets.
10. **Warnings and cautions first.** If a result has a caveat (vehicle confound, unlocked clock, partial overlap), state it before the number, not after.

## Relax (the 20%)

- The approved-word dictionary. Use the domain's real vocabulary (SMSP, roofline, p99); define it rather than avoid it.
- The ban on noun clusters. "memory-controller contention" is clearer than "contention at the controller of the memory".
- The absolute sentence-length limit when an equation or a cited table row needs the words.

## Note template (one note per unit)

```markdown
# <Unit title as in the source>

**Source:** <file/section/page>  ·  **Status in source:** <reviewed / drafted / pending, if stated>

## In one sentence
<The takeaway. If the reader stops here, this must be true and useful.>

## What it measures
<The metric, in prose, with units. Why this metric and not an obvious alternative.>

## How
<Setup in 3–6 sentences. Name the knobs that were swept and the ones that were held fixed.>

## Predicted
<The prediction and the reasoning behind it, including the equation if there is one, in words first.>

## Measured
<The result, with the numbers and their units. A small table if there are more than three numbers.>

## The gap, and what it means
<Where predicted and measured agree, where they do not, and the mechanism the source gives for the difference. If the source leaves it open, say so.>

## So what
<One or two sentences on why a practitioner in this area should care. Label this as interpretation if the source does not say it.>

## Terms introduced
<term — definition (added to glossary.md)>
```

## Glossary rules

`glossary.md` has one line per term: `**term** — definition (first used in <note>)`. Alphabetical. If the source uses an alternative name, append "Also called: …". Never define a term two ways.
