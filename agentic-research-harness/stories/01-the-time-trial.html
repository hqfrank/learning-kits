3Test the gate from both sides

The honest car passing proves the gate is not too tight. But only a caught decoy proves it is not too loose.

Decoys from the first campaign (tap a card)

A gate that never rejected a plausible cheat is untested. Popper ({{pub_popper_year}}): a claim is scientific only if some observation could refute it. Decoys are those observations, re-run on every rulebook version.

4No slip, no claim, and the coach keeps the failed plays

The judge is honest, but builders write fluent reports, and the next builder reads them. Fluency is not evidence.

5One round: plan, build in parallel, time one car at a time

Judge, slips and notes are in place, therefore the round can run without a person watching every lap.

6A new champion must beat the old one on every lap

The starter times each car {{harness_timed_calls}} times. Laps vary, therefore the crowning rule must not be fooled by luck.

A challenger is really no faster than the champion. Each drives {{harness_timed_calls}} laps. If the lower median lap wins, how often is the challenger crowned?

The rule and its priceCrown only if the challenger's worst lap beats the champion's best lap, both timed today. Under no real change that happens with probability 1 / C(40, 20) for 20 laps each. The price is power: a real gain smaller than the spread is rarely crowned. Changing the rule is the owner's decision (B7).

Where the race breaks

  • Nobody drives. A "lap" is one timed call, or one seed of a deterministic simulation; in the scheduling topic laps differ by workload seed, not by a driver.
  • Builders are not cheats. Most fast-but-wrong results are accidents, a bug that skips work, and decoys catch both.
  • A real race crowns the fastest lap. Here one fast lap never counts; the whole range does.
  • "Weather" is the measurement context: the host, its boot and the evaluator version, not the sky.
  • Gates are numeric limits (distance from the reference output, deadline misses, decision time), not posts beside a road.
