Cost grows linearly with researchers and rounds, the gain does not: each extra round buys less, and a broken meter hides the bill

An automated campaign (note B7) runs rounds of one planner and N researchers in parallel, each with a credit cap (note C1). Its cost per round is (planner credits + N × researcher credits) × dollars per credit × the share of the caps the agents actually spend. Its gain follows an illustrative diminishing-returns curve: each round closes a fixed share of the gap between the champion and an assumed floor, and parallel researchers overlap. A round counts only if its gain clears the minimum win ( %, the one-page proposal's minimum timing win, note B8); a smaller one is not promoted. Move the knobs and watch the dollars per millisecond climb.

Model choice of this page: the gain curve is invented. It is anchored to the first campaign's illustrative shape (c1_* keys, not the campaign's values: about ms after the hand-run rounds, about ms after automated rounds, for about USD) with researchers, and to an assumed ms floor. The credit caps and the dollar rate are the scheduling topic's configuration. Mixing the two is a page choice for illustration. Nothing on this page is a measurement or a forecast.
