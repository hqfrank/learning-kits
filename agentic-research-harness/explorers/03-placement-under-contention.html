On non-preemptive units the fastest unit is the wrong choice when it is busy: load-aware placement beats GPU-greedy as other chains load the GPU

A tiny discrete-event simulator in your browser, shaped like the scheduling topic's perception chain (note C2): capture (CPU) → preprocess (CPU or GPU) → detector and segmenter in parallel (each GPU or DLA) → tracker (CPU), released periodically. Other chains put their own jobs on the single GPU. Units are non-preemptive: a started job runs to the end. GPU-greedy places each job on its shortest-execution option (the topic's baseline placement). Balanced places it where it is predicted to finish first, given the work already queued (earliest-finish-time placement, HEFT). Dispatch is FIFO by ready time for both. The metric is the p95 end-to-end latency of the chain per seed; the page reports the median and the range over seeds, like the topic's records.

This is an illustrative page model, not the demonstration simulator: one chain plus a generic GPU background load, no deadlines for the other chains, so no gate. Job times are loaded from sched_node_ms (illustrative). The two models share the ms work floor, which this page reproduces exactly; its other numbers are its own and say nothing about a real robot computer.
