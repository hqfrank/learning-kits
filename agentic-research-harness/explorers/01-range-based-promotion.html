A median rule promotes a no-change candidate half the time; the disjoint-range rule almost never, and pays for it in power

The loop's promotion policy (note B7) makes a candidate champion only if its worst sample beats the champion's best sample, measured in the same context. Here a champion and a candidate each give n samples from a normal distribution (lower is better). Move the true shift and the spread and watch both rules: how often they promote when nothing changed (false promotion) and how often they promote a real gain (power). The bottom panel applies the rule to the demonstration run's own records.

Model choice of this page: samples are independent and normal, in arbitrary metric units with the champion's mean at 100. The no-change probabilities (0.5 and 1 / C(40, 20)) hold for any continuous distribution (note B7); the curves for a real shift depend on the normal assumption.
