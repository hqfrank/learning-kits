Weight each day by how many devices it rests on: weighted least squares gets the slope's error right, and the score follows

The series is the daily share of devices that emitted a metric, p̂t, from nt active devices (note A2). Its variance is p(1 − p)/nt, so a day with few devices is noisy. Ordinary least squares (OLS) gives every day the same say; weighted least squares (WLS) weights each day by nt. The slope is tested against a practical threshold s, and the one-sided p-value becomes a score = −10·log₁₀ p (note B4). All fleet sizes, shares and slopes are illustrative; the daily points are simulated on this page.

Log scale, 20 … 20 000. The note's small and large families are 100 and 10 000 devices (illustrative).
Weekend / outage days drop to n̄·(1 − v); a cohort ramps from n̄·(1 − v) to n̄·(1 + v).
Default = 3 rare-reason devices of 100 (illustrative, note A2).
Percentage points per week. Positive = getting worse (the direction filter keeps it).
H₀: β = s. The smallest slope worth an engineer's time; testing β = 0 flags every tiny drift in a big fleet.
1 = pure sampling noise. φ > 1 multiplies the variance (households, weather, drift), which the weights do not know about. My model, not from the notes.

Parameters & sources

Every constant is loaded from the inlined params block (also shipped as params.json), copied from ../constants.json. public = cited; illustrative = a round teaching number. WLS weights use the pooled share p̄ over the series (note B4, step 3). "True" standard errors are computed from the simulation's own pt, nt and φ; "reported" ones are what each fit would print from the data. The p-value uses a normal approximation for the slope (note B4, step 4). Self-tests: not run.
