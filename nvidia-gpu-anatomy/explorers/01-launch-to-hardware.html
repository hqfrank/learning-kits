A kernel launch does not fill an SM — the tightest of five limits does

You choose a grid of blocks of threads. The hardware packs whole blocks onto each streaming multiprocessor (SM) until the first of five per-SM limits runs out: resident warps, resident threads, resident blocks, the register file, or shared memory. Move the sliders; watch which limit binds, how many blocks are admitted, and the resulting occupancy. Occupancy is opportunity to issue — not issue rate (note 03).

SMSP view. Each SM has 4 sub-partitions (SMSPs); the hardware spreads a block's warps across them, so warps-per-SMSP ≈ resident warps ÷ 4. A sub-partition issues at most one warp instruction per cycle, so its scheduler needs several resident warps to hide latency — that is what occupancy supplies (notes 01, 03).
