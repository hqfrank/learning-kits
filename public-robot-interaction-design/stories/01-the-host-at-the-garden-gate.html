A good host greets first, listens only after you answer, looks at whoever it is answering, and fails so quietly that you never notice.A public robot speaks first, opens its microphone only after a visitor action, aims its head at the addressee, and degrades without a visible seam.

A child-sized robot at a busy public stand meets strangers: no instructions, a few seconds of attention, groups, noise, children. Think of it as a small host at a garden gate. Every success must need nothing from the guest, and every failure must be invisible to them. Flip to Engineer view for the real names.

1Greet first; lift the trumpet only when they answer

The host may speak to anyone. It may listen only after the guest does something visible.

The host has just waved and said hello to a passer-by. When may it lift the ear trumpet?

2One path at a time; a no is a no

Inside the gate, {{offerings_illustrative}} paths leave one little plaza illustrative. A host, not a vending machine: it never reads out the menu.

3The hourglass sets the queue

The visit is a row of timed beats. They add up to the dwell time; add the reset and you have a cycle. The cycle decides how many guests the gate can welcome in an hour.

The reset (close the gate, wipe the slate, next guest steps up) takes {{reset_turnover_illustrative}} s. The guest has already left by then. Cut it by 10 s: how many more guests per hour?

4Who spoke? Panels gate, lips choose

But guests come in groups, and the nearest one is often not the one talking. The host hears which fence panel a voice came through; it sees faces only inside the hedge.

A radio plays behind the hedge. The parent in front of the gate is silent. Who does the host say spoke?

5Look at whoever you answer, and glide

Therefore the head follows the evidence, not the loudest or nearest guest. Gaze works across a loud room in silence, and a wrong turn is easy to see.

6The host hears himself

But the trumpet stays up while the host talks, so guests can interrupt. Then it also hears the host: his voice, and his clogs every time he nods.

The greeting is now shorter: {{proactive_greeting_illustrative}} s. The guest replies about {{turn_gap_typical}} ms after it ends, as people do. What happens?

7The guest must never tell

Therefore failure is planned as rungs. Each rung is a whole visit that still ends with the flower, and the guest cannot tell which rung is running. Tap a card.

Degrade the richness, never the truth. No "sorry, I can't hear you". No celebrating a photo that failed, and no answer the guest never gave. Every wait has a bound: no flower taken → "no rush" → one wait of {{take_timer_illustrative}} s illustrative → retract. C1

Where the garden breaks

  • A real host remembers faces. The robot must never claim to remember anyone: after the visit closes, a returning person is a stranger again.
  • An ear trumpet is the hearing. The robot's microphones are always there; "trumpet down" is a promise kept by an audio gate, and a content-free voice detector runs before consent.
  • Fence panels have sharp edges. A real direction bin thrashes within a turn, and it can point at the robot's own voice or a reflection off glass.
  • A person barely hears their own footsteps. The robot's processed microphone channel amplifies its own motors (gain wind-up), so the clatter can be louder than a guest.
  • The hedge stays put. The head camera's view turns with the head, so every camera bearing must be added to the measured neck angle.
  • A hidden gardener sounds like a trick. At the stand the operator is a planned floor that makes autonomy safe to attempt, and has a script for privacy questions.
